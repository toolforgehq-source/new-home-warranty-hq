# Builder Warranty vs Home Warranty

The names sound alike, but they are different things.

| | Builder warranty | Home warranty (service contract) |
|---|---|---|
| Comes from | The builder, sometimes backed by a third-party warranty company | A service-contract company |
| Cost | Comes with new construction | Costs extra; you choose whether to buy |
| Typically covers | Workmanship and materials of permanent parts of the home (e.g., plumbing, electrical) | Repair/replacement of items like appliances and air conditioning |
| Typical home | Newly built | Typically existing homes |
Source: FTC, "Warranties for New Homes."

## Key points from the FTC
- Most newly built homes come with a builder warranty.
- A "home warranty" is really a service contract, and the FTC says service contracts are not warranties.
- Builder warranties generally don't cover things like household appliances or components covered by a manufacturer's warranty. Read yours.
- FHA and VA require builders to buy third-party warranties for buyers with FHA/VA-financed new homes (per FTC).

## Where does insurance fit?
Homeowners insurance is a third, separate product. Insurance is not covered in detail here; ask your insurer.

## Which do you need?
New-construction owners should first understand their builder warranty: what's covered, for how long, and how to make a claim. Whether to buy a service contract is your choice; the FTC advises reading terms closely.
