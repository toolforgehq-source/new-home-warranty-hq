# 11-Month Warranty Inspection: What It Is, What It Costs, and When to Book

Most builder warranties give you about a year to report problems with workmanship and materials. The FTC notes that coverage on most components "usually expires after the first year." An 11-month inspection is a walk-through of your home, usually by a professional home inspector, that finds issues while you can still ask the builder to fix them. (Check your own warranty document for your exact dates.)

## What is it?
A visual inspection, typically a few weeks before the one-year mark, that produces a written list of defects (cracks, nail pops, drainage, doors, windows, plumbing and electrical function, and more) that you can send to your builder.

## When should I book it?
Plan for month 9 or 10 so the report lands before your deadline. Inspectors can book out, and the builder needs time to schedule repairs. Don't wait for month 11 and a day. Your warranty, not this guide, sets the deadline.

## What does it cost?
Prices vary by inspector, home size and region. We don't publish a price range here because we haven't verified a representative one. Get quotes from two or three local inspectors who specifically offer builder-warranty (11-month) inspections, and ask what the report includes and how fast you'll get it.

## DIY vs pro
| | DIY | Professional |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free | Varies; get local quotes |
| Strengths | Convenient, you know the home | Trained eye, thorough report |
| Limits | Easy to miss hidden issues | Cost; availability |
Many owners do a DIY pass at month 9 and decide whether to hire a pro. Use our free [11-month checklist](/guides/11-month-checklist).

## After the inspection
1. Sort findings: safety first, then items you believe your warranty covers.
2. Submit them to your builder in writing, following your warranty's instructions. The FTC recommends putting requests in writing and keeping records, and suggests certified mail with return receipt for proof.
3. Keep dated photos and copies of everything.
