# Builder Not Fixing Warranty Items? What to Do Next

## 1. Re-read your warranty
Check that the problem is covered and note the time limit for that component (FTC).

## 2. Put it in writing
Even if there is a hotline, the FTC advises putting repair requests in writing, optionally by certified mail with return receipt, so you can prove delivery. Use your warranty's submission method too. Our free templates: [/guides/builder-warranty-letters](/guides/builder-warranty-letters).

## 3. Document everything
Dated photos, short video, a log of each call, email and visit. A simple table works: date, who, what was said, what's next.

## 4. Follow up on a schedule
Pick a cadence and stick to it, e.g. a written follow-up if no acknowledgment after about a week [ESTIMATE; your warranty may set its own response times].

## 5. Use the warranty's dispute process
The FTC says many new-home warranties offer or require mediation then arbitration. Mediation means a neutral helps you and the company talk, but agreement is voluntary. Arbitration is usually binding and may cost up to several thousand dollars (FTC). FHA/VA-financed buyers with third-party warranty claims may choose arbitration or court (FTC).

## 6. Call outside help
Your state or local builders' board (FTC suggestion), and for questions about your rights, a licensed attorney in your state. We can't give legal advice.

## Don't miss the deadline
Coverage on most components usually ends after year one, so report problems before then. See: "[What to do when your builder warranty is expiring](/guides/builder-warranty-expiring)."

New Home Warranty HQ is a software tool for logging issues, photos and requests in one place; it doesn't guarantee a builder will respond. It is not a law firm.
